The medal says 21km. My legs would tell you a different story — something closer to "13km race, 8km funeral march."

![Nha Trang Half Marathon medal]({{"/assets/images/2026-08-09-vnexpress-nha-trang-half-marathon-2026-medal.jpg" | relative_url}})


This was supposed to be my redemption race. First marathon was in the books back in May at Can Gio, base-building had been going well, and I flew up to Nha Trang with a plan: run it clean, run it smart, walk away with proof that the last few months of treadmill grinding actually meant something.

Instead I walked. Literally, for the last third of the race.

## The good part first

Because there was one, and it deserves credit before I roast myself.

The first 13km were genuinely strong. Splits from Coros:

Best kilometer of the day: 7:03. That's a solid tempo effort, and it felt controlled while it was happening. Ground contact time, cadence, stride length — all of it was clean and consistent through that stretch. If the race had ended at 13km, this would be a very different, much shorter blog post.

## Then the wheels came off

Here's the same table for the second half:

| km | pace | avg HR |
|----|------|--------|
| 14 | 9:14 | 156 |
| 15 | 9:28 | 153 |
| 16 | 11:02 | 137 |
| 17 | 10:31 | 137 |
| 18 | 10:05 | 137 |
| 19 | 11:59 | 127 |
| 20 | 11:43 | 121 |
| 21 | 11:21 | 122 |

Look at that HR column. It's not fatigue in the "I'm still trying but slowing down" sense — HR cratering from the 170s down into the 120s while pace also collapses is the signature of a body that has downshifted into survival mode. That's walking HR, not struggling-to-run HR. Sharp lateral pain in my left knee and something not right in my right ankle showed up right around km14, and that was that. No amount of willpower fixes a joint that's telling you to stop.

Final numbers: 21.46km in 3:07:44, average pace 8:45/km, average HR 152, Training Load 439. Coros flagged the session as "Tempo" focus with Aerobic TE 5.1 and Anaerobic TE 3.4 — which, in plain terms, confirms what the splits already show. I went out at a real threshold effort for a distance and duration my legs weren't ready to hold. The engine was fine. The chassis wasn't.

## The highlight nobody wants but everyone needs

Somewhere in that grim second half, on top of the joint pain, my stomach decided to join the mutiny. First time in my running life I've felt the genuine, unmistakable warning signs of runner's trot — the kind where you start doing math on how far away the next aid station is instead of thinking about pace. Found a mobile WC booth with what felt like seconds to spare. Genuinely one of the more relieved moments — in every sense — of my racing career so far.

I'm laughing about it now. I was not laughing about it at the time.

## Still a good day, weirdly

Objectively this race went sideways. But I finished it, I got the medal, and I got a very clean dataset showing exactly where and why things broke down — which is worth more to me long-term than a clean finish would have been. A half marathon that goes perfectly teaches you nothing. This one handed me a diagnosis.

## Lessons learned

- **First 13km pace was a mistake, not a strength.** Sub-7:20 with HR pinned at 165-172 is threshold effort. That's a 10K pace, not a half marathon pace, and my body sent the bill exactly when threshold efforts always send the bill — somewhere past the hour-and-a-half mark.
- **Flat feet + all that road pounding is catching up with me.** The knee/ankle pain lines up with gait issues I've been meaning to get looked at. Time to actually book the physio/sports med referral instead of nodding at the idea.
- **Treadmill training doesn't fully prepare you for road impact.** Incline treadmill work builds the engine, but the repetitive road pounding on pavement is a different stimulus than a belt. Need more outdoor volume before the next race, not less.
- **HR data doesn't lie, even when your ego wants it to.** The moment HR crashed alongside pace was the moment to stop pushing pace and start managing damage. I basically did that by necessity, but next time I want to read that signal earlier and adjust on purpose.
- **Carry runner's trot mitigation seriously from now on.** Whatever I ate/drank pre-race and during is getting reviewed. Getting lucky with a WC booth once is not a plan.
- **The half marathon is still the right distance for me.** Nothing about this race changes that. If anything, a bad half is a cheaper lesson than a bad full would've been — I can be back racing another one within a month or two instead of needing another five-month buildup.

Next up: get the gait actually assessed, rebuild the aerobic base properly, and come back to race a half the way it's supposed to be raced — even pacing, not a 13km sprint followed by an 8km apology.
