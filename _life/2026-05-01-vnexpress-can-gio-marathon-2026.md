First marathon. VnExpress Marathon Green Paradise, out along the Rung Sac mangrove corridor toward Can Gio and back — a point-to-point-and-back course, mostly flat, mostly exposed, starting in the dark specifically so you're not fighting full sun for all 42km. Race organizers weren't messing around with that 4 AM gun.

## What Coros actually recorded

Final numbers: 42.74km in 6:31:07, average pace 9:09/km, average HR 163, elevation gain 57m / loss 67m — basically flat, so no excuses there. Training Load 1093. Coros rated the effort "Poor" performance, "Very Tired" perceived effort, and filed it under "Base" training focus, which is a very polite way of saying I ran the first half like it mattered and the second half like it was punishing me for that decision.

Here's the race in 5km blocks:

| segment | pace | avg HR |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5km | 7:41/km | 172 |
| 5–10km | 7:42/km | 177 |
| 10–15km | 7:50/km | 174 |
| 15–20km | 7:37/km | 172 |
| 20–25km | 9:11/km | 162 |
| 25–30km | 9:47/km | 158 |
| 30–35km | 10:42/km | 155 |
| 35–40km | 10:22/km | 154 |
| 40–42.74km | 13:26/km | 145 |

That table tells the whole story without me needing to add much. Twenty kilometers at 172-177 bpm and sub-7:50 pace is not marathon pacing — that's half marathon race effort, held for the length of an actual half marathon, before I'd even reached the halfway point of the actual race. Best kilometer of the day was 7:00 flat, and it happened somewhere in that opening stretch, which in hindsight is exactly the problem. I ran my best kilometer while I still had 22km left to run.

The back half is the bill for that. Pace fell off in a straight line from km20 onward — 9:11, then 9:47, then over 10:00/km for two straight blocks — and the last stretch into the finish was 13:26/km, which at that point is a shuffle-walk with occasional jogging convincing itself it's still running. HR actually dropping through all of this (177 down to 145) while pace also collapsed is the same signature I'd later see again at Nha Trang: not "still trying and slowing down," but the body pulling the plug and negotiating a ceasefire.

## First marathon, first real lesson in pacing

Nobody warns you clearly enough that 42.2km punishes front-loaded effort so much more severely than 21.1km does. In a half, going out hot for the first 10-13km is often recoverable — you suffer, but you can usually hang on and finish reasonably. In a full, the same mistake compounds over twice the distance and the crash isn't linear, it's a cliff. I found that cliff right around km20, and spent the next 22km paying for the first 20.

Credit where it's due: I finished. 42.74km, under the cutoff, medal earned, first marathon in the books. That's not nothing, and on the day it felt like a genuine accomplishment crossing that line regardless of how ugly the last 10km had been.

But sitting with it after, I came away thinking the marathon is a distance that demands a level of pacing discipline and specific training I hadn't built yet — and honestly, may not be the distance I want to keep chasing. Executing a full marathon well is a real craft, and it takes buildup and patience most people, myself included, don't necessarily have the bandwidth for more than once or twice a year. A half marathon lets you race more often, recover faster, and still get the full teeth of a hard effort without needing to solve the fueling and pacing puzzle of 42km all at once.

## Lessons learned

- **Even splits beat fast starts, every single time, at this distance.** Running 172-177 bpm through 20km of a marathon is racing a half marathon by accident. The back half doesn't forgive that the way a half marathon sometimes will.
- **"Feels controlled" isn't the same as "is sustainable."** The first 20km felt fine in the moment. HR data says otherwise in hindsight — I was well above an aerobic marathon effort long before I noticed anything was wrong.
- **Fueling and pacing need a real plan for anything past ~25km.** I didn't have one. The wheels came off right around where a proper fueling/pacing strategy would've started mattering most.
- **A 4 AM start is a gift — don't waste the cool hours going out too fast.** The early start was there to protect me from the heat in the back half. Instead I spent the cool window burning matches I needed later.
- **This distance taught me the half marathon is where I actually want to live.** Not because 42.2km broke me — it didn't, I finished — but because the juice-to-squeeze ratio favors racing halves more often over chasing marathons rarely and recovering from each one for months.

Would I run another full marathon? Probably, eventually. But not before I respect this distance a lot more than I did on race morning.
